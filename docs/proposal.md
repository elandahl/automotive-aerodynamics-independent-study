# Independent Study Project Proposal

**Course context:** Independent study in Physics  
**Focus:** Automotive aerodynamics for industrial design  
**Duration:** Ten weeks  
**Student background:** Completed the full introductory college physics sequence (algebra-based); pursuing a Physics minor at DePaul University  
**Faculty:** Physics professor (mentor for physics content, experimental design, and quantitative analysis)

---

## Project focus

This independent study will focus on the effects of aerodynamics on the performance and design of cars, specifically how airflow interacts with a vehicle at different speeds and angles. The student will investigate how the design of specific car components—such as **spoilers, wings, splitters, and diffusers**—influences the forces acting on a vehicle.

Throughout the ten weeks, the student will develop understanding of the principles of aerodynamics by researching different automotive designs and studying how these components are used in modern performance vehicles. That research will inform the student’s own **modular aerodynamic designs**.

## Physical testing plan

To physically test the research, the student will design and build a **scale vehicle** and an **aerodynamic testing platform**. The vehicle will use a modular system that allows interchangeable spoilers, wings, splitters, and diffusers. Different aerodynamic configurations can then be tested while keeping the vehicle body consistent.

The student will test different wing shapes, angles, and configurations under controlled airflow conditions and use **sensors** to collect data on how these changes affect the vehicle. **CAD software** will be used to design and develop the vehicle and its interchangeable aerodynamic components.

## Faculty learning goals (physics)

Beyond design exploration, this independent study is structured so the student can:

1. Connect algebra-based introductory physics (force, energy, pressure, waves/oscillations as relevant) to **fluid-force models** used in vehicle aerodynamics.
2. Predict, measure, and interpret **drag** and **downforce (negative lift)** for modular aero parts.
3. Use **dimensional analysis and Reynolds-number thinking** so scale-model results are interpreted honestly.
4. Practice **experimental design**: controls, uncertainty, repeatability, and clear communication of results.
5. Build a portfolio suitable for a Physics minor pathway: quantitative reasoning applied to a design problem.
