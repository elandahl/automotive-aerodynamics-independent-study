# Reading & reference list

Prioritize understanding and application over collecting PDFs. Start with ★ items.

## Physics foundations (algebra-friendly)

- ★ OpenStax *University Physics* or *College Physics* — chapters on fluids (pressure, Bernoulli, continuity). Use as refreshers, then return to course notes for car-specific caveats.
- Introductory notes in this repo: [`physics/`](../physics/README.md).

## Aerodynamics / automotive (concepts)

- ★ Katz, J. — *Race Car Aerodynamics* (accessible engineering treatment of wings, underbodies, and coefficients; skim math that exceeds algebra, keep the physical stories and figures).
- Manufacturer / motorsport technical explainers (Porsche, Mercedes F1 festival talks, SAE student papers) — use for design vocabulary; verify claims against your measurements.
- NASA / educational airfoil glossaries for AoA and stall language (aircraft-oriented; translate signs for downforce).

## Experimental practice

- ★ Any first-year physics lab manual chapter on uncertainty and significant figures.
- Load-cell and anemometer vendor application notes (calibration).

## Design process

- Industrial design case studies of automotive exterior surfacing — pair each aesthetic choice with a hypothesized pressure/separation effect (Week 4 honesty captions).

## DePaul Physics minor planning

- Review current DePaul Physics minor requirements with the mentor and department advisor.
- Typical bridge after this IS: finish calculus as needed → intermediate mechanics → electives involving fluids, computation, or experimental methods.

## Citation hygiene

When the final report quotes a \(C_D\) from the web, cite it and state whether it is full-scale coast-down, CFD, or marketing. Your own coefficients should be labeled with **Re**, **\(A\)**, and apparatus limits.
