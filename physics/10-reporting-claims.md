# Reporting claims

Week 10 adds no new force law. It fixes which sentences the earlier weeks support.

## Order

Design question and metric, then the model ($q$, coefficients, signs, Re), then the apparatus (axis, calibration, tare, $v$), then results, then what failed to match, then the part you would keep.

The metric is the Week 1 sentence. It is not revised so that the favorite config wins.

## Claim ladder

| Claim | Evidence it still needs |
|-------|-------------------------|
| The force changed | Calibration in newtons, and a tare |
| $C_L$ or $C_D$ changed | Measured $v$, frozen $A$, same $q$ |
| This config ranks higher here | Replicates, one variable at a time |
| The road car has this $C_D$ | Matched Re. This course does not claim it. |

Week 5’s three-sentence disclaimer is part of the physics section and part of any caption that could be read as a road-car number.

## A paragraph shape

State $q$ and $A$. State the solo changes and the pair. State the residual in words (“less downforce than the sum”) or state that it sits inside the spread. Name the champion under the pre-written rule, including the drag or downforce given up. Close with the Re limit.

Lecture numbers for practice only: wing $\Delta F_z=-1.2\,\mathrm{N}$, diffuser $-0.8\,\mathrm{N}$, both $-1.5\,\mathrm{N}$, residual $0.5\,\mathrm{N}$ less downforce than the sum. A $1.30\,\mathrm{N}$ drag cap selects the wing-only row of the Week 9 table. Those values are not a measured car.

## Plots

Each figure needs config ids, the mean, the replicate spread, and $v$ or Re plus $A$ in the caption. The talk (10–12 minutes) spends about four minutes on two or three of those figures and about two minutes on the champion plus the scaling sentence.

## Course stance

A ranking you measured, with the limit stated, is the finished result. A full-scale coefficient, a stall you cannot see above the noise, and a metric invented in the conclusion are not.
