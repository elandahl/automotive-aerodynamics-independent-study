# Week 1 — Forces, free-body diagrams, and project goals

## Learning targets

- Restate the project in **measurable** physics language (forces, not vibes).
- Draw FBDs for a car: weight, normal forces, drag, downforce, drive/brake forces.
- Explain why downforce can increase cornering grip **without** adding engine power.

## Physics checkpoint

1. A 1500 kg car sits at rest. What is the total normal force from the ground (ignore aero)?  
2. Same car at speed develops 2000 N of downforce (net aero force into the road) and 800 N of drag. Redraw the FBD in free cruise (constant velocity). What balances drag? What happens to tire normal force?  
3. Industrial-design prompt: In one paragraph, explain “aero grip” to a non-physics classmate using only Newton’s laws and normal force.

## Design / build tasks

- List modular parts to build (spoiler, wing, splitter, diffuser) and what each is *claimed* to do.
- Define 2–3 **success metrics** (e.g., measurable change in vertical load at fixed airspeed; repeatability within X%).
- Photograph or sketch the intended test platform.

## Deliverables

- [ ] Checkpoint answers  
- [ ] One-page project brief (goals + metrics)  
- [ ] Parts list + labeling scheme (WING-01, SPLIT-02, …)

## Mentor notes

Watch for “downforce makes the car lighter” language—correct toward **increases normal force / available friction**, not reduced mass.
